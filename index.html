About

Why this work exists

I work at the intersection of industrial engineering and AI — with hands-on shop floor experience in Indian manufacturing and a professional focus on hyperscaler relationships at PTC. This program is how I explore the gap between those two worlds.

The core thesis: LLM agents can operationalize IE theory, but the degree depends on reasoning architecture, information richness, and problem framing — and these effects are separable and measurable.

Each experiment takes a foundational IE concept — a supply chain dynamic, a maintenance framework, an inventory model — and turns it into a controlled simulation environment where LLM agents make decisions. The goal is to understand failure modes and conditions for reliability, not just capability demos.

All experiments are independent, use personal compute, and involve entirely fictional scenarios. Preregistration on OSF ensures hypotheses are committed before data collection.

Methodology

How experiments are designed

01

Analytical control baselines

Every experiment pairs LLM agent performance against a non-LLM analytical benchmark — not just model-vs-model. Deviation from theory is the signal.

02

OSF preregistration

Hypotheses and designs are committed publicly on OSF before data collection begins. The timestamp is the point — not whether the hypothesis is confirmed.

03

Controlled simulation environments

Synthetic but calibrated parameters derived from public literature. All scenarios are entirely fictional with no proprietary data involved.

04

Multi-model comparison

Experiments compare across model tiers and reasoning architectures, with 50–100 replications per cell to support statistical inference.
