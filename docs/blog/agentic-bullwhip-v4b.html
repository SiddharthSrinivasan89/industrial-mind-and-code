Better classifications, similar ordering outcomes

Siddharth Srinivasan · April 2026

Can better interpretation of seasonal and disruption signals improve supply-chain orders when the model only chooses from five labels? Classification improved with context, but the resulting order variance remained close across the tested models.

The question and the models

I tested gpt-4.1-mini, o4-mini, phi4:14b, and nemotron-super 120B in an intent-classification architecture. The model selected a direction label, from strong decrease to strong increase. Software mapped that label to one of five safety-stock multipliers between 0.80 and 1.30, then an Order-Up-To formula calculated the order.

This let the model interpret information while keeping numerical execution in ordinary code. The research question was whether better labels would improve the operation of the chain—not simply whether the responses would be well formed.

The data and method

The fictional three-tier chain used 36 months of synthetic demand shaped around Indian automotive seasonality. It also included simulated pandemic, geopolitical-conflict, and port-strike events. Lead times and the proportion of orders fulfilled varied randomly. The event headlines supplied to the models were part of the simulation, not a live-news feed.

Each model received numbers alone, calendar and seasonal context, or context plus event text. The two frontier models ran twenty repetitions per condition; the local models ran ten. Baselines ran one hundred times because the environment itself was stochastic.

The principal measure was chain OVAR, the average of each tier's order-variance-to-demand-variance ratio. Above one means amplification. Stockouts, directional classification accuracy, and compliance with the allowed labels supplied separate views of performance. A correct direction label is not the same as a stable ordering decision.

What we found

All twelve model-condition means fell between 1.726 and 1.780 OVAR. The entire range, 0.054, was smaller than the within-condition standard deviations of 0.088–0.133. These close observed values motivated the term “Equaliser Effect”; no formal equivalence test established that the conditions were interchangeable.

The best observed model condition was phi4:14b with context at 1.726. Exponential smoothing achieved 1.185, while the formula-only Order-Up-To baseline achieved 1.767. The models therefore clustered near the latter rather than closing the gap to smoothing.

There was a service trade-off: exponential smoothing averaged 89.62 stockout tier-periods per run, compared with 86.95 for Order-Up-To and 84.7–87.0 across the model conditions. Its lower variance did not mean it won on every operational measure.

Blind directional accuracy ranged from 0.41 to 0.48. Calendar context raised it to approximately 0.72–0.80; event text brought some conditions as high as 0.84. Those gains did not translate into comparable OVAR gains. In every model, the event-text condition had slightly higher OVAR than its calendar-context condition.

The hypotheses that an AI condition would beat smoothing, that event text would reduce OVAR, and that it would improve direction accuracy during disruption periods by at least ten percentage points were not supported. The compliance threshold and the hypothesis that world events materially changed OVAR passed. Removing events in the matched phi4 conditions increased OVAR to about 2.08–2.12, showing that this variance ratio responds to the demand environment as well as the ordering policy.

A separate instruction to default to neutral shifted mean OVAR by −0.015 to +0.005 across six matched cells. Neutral still triggered the ordering formula at a multiplier of 1.0; it did not mean “place no order.” This is an important distinction between what a label sounds like and what the software actually executes.

Limits and next steps

The clustering is consistent with the fixed lookup and downstream formula limiting the influence of classification. It does not, by itself, prove that perfect classification could never help. Stochastic supply conditions, one synthetic demand profile, and one lookup design bound the finding.

The next experiment tested that unresolved question directly by replacing the language model with perfect simulation-derived labels and deterministic rules. V4b motivates that test; it should not borrow its conclusion in advance.
