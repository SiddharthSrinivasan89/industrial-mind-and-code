Letting AI adjust the safety-stock buffer

Siddharth Srinivasan · April 2026

Could AI help with ordering if it adjusted only the inventory buffer and left the arithmetic to a formula? I tested that narrower role. Every model condition produced more order variance than a fixed buffer multiplier, although stockouts were lower than that control.

Adjusting the buffer, not the whole order

Safety stock is inventory held above a forecast to protect against shortages. I tested whether gpt-4.1-mini, o4-mini, and the local nemotron-3-super:120b model could use seasonal information to adjust that buffer without destabilising orders.

Each model chose a multiplier between 0.5 and 3.0. An Order-Up-To formula combined the forecast, the scaled safety buffer, and the current inventory position to calculate the order. The multiplier affected only safety stock: choosing 1.2 meant a 20% larger buffer, not necessarily a 20% larger order.

Separating the model's contribution from the formula

The fictional three-tier chain served one product over 25 months of synthetic demand shaped around Indian automotive seasonality. Lead times were fixed at one month, with no disruption events or stochastic supply delays.

The Azure models ran twenty repetitions per information condition; the stored Nemotron results contain ten. Blind supplied numerical state alone. Context added calendar and seasonal flags. Stateful also supplied the last three periods of demand, orders, multiplier choices, backlog, and stockouts. If an answer could not be parsed, the multiplier fell back to a neutral 1.0.

Two deterministic controls answered different questions. Exponential smoothing provided a simple reference policy. The hybrid control used the same ordering formula as the models, but held the multiplier at 1.0. That second comparison isolated the contribution of changing the multiplier within this design.

I measured chain OVAR—the average across tiers of order variance divided by demand variance—alongside stockouts and inventory. Below one means damping; above one means amplification. Reading these outcomes together avoids mistaking smoother orders for better service.

All nine model conditions had higher OVAR than the fixed-multiplier control. They also had fewer stockouts than that control, so there was a trade-off rather than deterioration on every measure. Exponential smoothing, however, recorded both lower OVAR and fewer stockouts than every model condition.

Context and history did not meet the improvement threshold

Context increased OVAR for Nemotron and gpt-4.1-mini and slightly reduced it for o4-mini. History improved Nemotron and gpt-4.1-mini relative to their context conditions, but only by 0.0783 and 0.2537 OVAR. The reported hypothesis required an improvement of at least 0.5 for at least two models; those gains did not meet it. For o4-mini, history made the result worse.

All four stated hypotheses were rejected. The highest seasonal multiplier-pattern score was 0.3977, below the required 0.50. Plausible explanations about busy seasons did not translate into the required operational performance.

A reason to test a different interface

The finding applies to a continuous multiplier attached to this ordering formula. It does not establish that model-assisted control always fails or that memory is inherently harmful. One synthetic demand profile, fixed lead times, the tested models, and an unchanged smoothing parameter bound the result. The hypotheses were not formally pre-registered.

The next design replaced the free-form multiplier with a small set of labels mapped to fixed values. Removing numerical calibration from the model's task was a question worth testing—not an improvement already established by this experiment.

Conclusion

Giving AI control of the safety-stock multiplier did not produce steadier orders than leaving the multiplier fixed. The model conditions reduced stockouts relative to that control, but exponential smoothing achieved both lower variance and fewer stockouts than every model condition. The tested buffer-adjustment design therefore did not meet its operational targets; the result does not establish that memory or model-assisted control is inherently ineffective.
