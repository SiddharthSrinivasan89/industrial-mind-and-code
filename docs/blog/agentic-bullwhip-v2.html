Supply Chain · Experiment Writeup

Agentic Bullwhip Effect — Version 2

Every heuristic outperformed every LLM configuration. Exponential smoothing beat state-of-the-art reasoning models 8× on order variance and 8× on stockouts — simultaneously.

Date March 2026
Experiment Agentic Bullwhip Effect Version 2
Design 2×2 Factorial · 20 runs · 11,520 LLM calls

Abstract

The bullwhip effect is a foundational supply chain problem: small fluctuations in consumer demand cause progressively larger swings in orders further up the chain, leading to excess inventory, stockouts, and wasted capacity. Version 2 asks a harder question than Version 1 — not which AI configuration performs best, but whether any LLM configuration outperforms a simple rule-based heuristic at all.

Four LLM configurations — lightweight and reasoning models, frontier and local — were tested against three deterministic heuristic baselines across 20 independent runs per condition, totalling 11,520 LLM calls over a 25-month synthetic demand series calibrated to Indian automotive seasonal patterns.

Every heuristic outperformed every LLM configuration on both order variance and service level simultaneously. This is not a tradeoff result — the LLMs were strictly worse on both dimensions at once.

Key findings

What I found

  1. Every heuristic outperformed every LLM on both OVAR and stockouts simultaneously. This is not a tradeoff. The LLMs were strictly dominated across both primary metrics in every configuration tested.
  2. The gap is not marginal. Exponential smoothing achieved chain-average OVAR 0.54 with 5 stockout periods. The best LLM (local phi4:14b, blind) achieved OVAR 4.33 with 41 stockouts — 8× worse on both dimensions at once.
  3. The Ancillary tier (middle) is consistently the most amplified — every model, every configuration. It absorbs already-distorted orders from the OEM tier and amplifies them further, the textbook bullwhip accumulation point.
  4. Context had opposite effects depending on model and backend. For frontier gpt-4.1-mini, adding business context reduced OVAR marginally (4.70 → 4.47, Δ=0.23 — below the MPRD threshold). For local phi4:14b, the same context was dramatically worse: chain OVAR jumped from 4.33 to 6.35, with the Ancillary tier hitting 10.82 ± 8.14 — some runs were catastrophic outliers.
  5. Reasoning models showed no ordering advantage over lightweight models. The 116B gpt-oss:120b produced results statistically indistinguishable from gpt-4.1-mini in blind conditions. o4-mini generated over 1 million reasoning tokens — none of it translated to better ordering outcomes.
  6. The failure is structural, not a prompting problem. Each agent sees only the current period's state with no memory of prior decisions. LLMs also optimise for plausible text, not numerically precise outcomes. The heuristic doesn't reason — it applies the formula it was built for. That is its advantage.

H1 was rejected by the widest margin — 8× worse on OVAR and 8× worse on stockouts simultaneously. H2 and H3 were directionally consistent but failed to reach the MPRD threshold. H4 and H5 show reasoning models performed no better than lightweight — the contrast was negative in both conditions. H7 confirms the local/frontier divergence under context was substantial, not noise.

Discussion

Why the formula won

Why LLMs fail to smooth

The bullwhip failure is structural. Each agent sees only the current period — no memory of what it ordered previously, no causal chain connecting its past decisions to the current supply chain state. Without this, there is no mechanism for self-correction. A stateless agent that ordered too much last period arrives at the next period without knowing it did. The supply chain inherits the consequences; the agent does not.

The second reason is that quantity estimation is not what LLMs optimise for. LLMs reason about supply chains the way a textbook does — they can describe the bullwhip effect, but that doesn't mean they can avoid causing it. Every period, an LLM makes a fresh judgement call on a number. That discretion generates variance. A deterministic formula has zero within-run variance by construction. The OVAR comparison is, in a sense, a comparison between the variance introduced by formula (≈0) versus the variance introduced by LLM discretion (substantial).

Context as a noise amplifier

The local lightweight model result deserves specific attention. Adding business context to phi4:14b did not help — it destabilised an otherwise rigid model. The Ancillary tier standard deviation of 8.14 OVAR across 20 runs means some runs were near-normal while others were extreme outliers. Context likely triggered an aggressive ordering interpretation in certain runs that then propagated as an amplified shock upstream. The rigid blind model, repeating the same order every run, at least produced predictable underperformance.

For frontier models, context showed a small directional improvement — Δ=0.23 for gpt-4.1-mini — but well below the MPRD threshold. The signal is there; the magnitude is not sufficient to justify the additional prompt engineering cost in this task class.

What this means for deployment

A hybrid approach is needed. LLMs alone are not ready for stateless, zero-shot ordering decisions — making quantity calls each period with no memory, no fine-tuning, and no outcome feedback. But the data points toward where they do belong. Frontier models showed marginal improvement with business context — suggesting some sensitivity to conditions. The heuristics won on execution every time — the math is reliable where LLM quantity judgement is not.

The natural split: LLM reads conditions and adjusts inputs, formula executes the order.

Use LLMs to interpret seasonal signals, flag anomalies, and modify heuristic parameters — then let the formula handle execution. This is where the experiment's data points.

Methodology note

All scenarios, companies, products, and supply chain structures in this experiment are entirely fictional and constructed for experimental purposes. No proprietary, confidential, or employer-owned data was used. This experiment is intentionally narrow: single product, fixed 1-month lead times, no supplier disruptions, no unstructured context, no multi-objective tradeoffs. Results reflect this specific configuration. The correct scope of any conclusion is: "LLM agents do not outperform simple blind heuristics in a stylised single-product replenishment task with fixed lead times and no unstructured context." Local compute runs on an Asus Ascent GX10 with NVIDIA GB10 Blackwell SoC — personally owned. Azure and other AI subscriptions are personal in nature.
