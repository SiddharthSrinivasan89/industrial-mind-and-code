Comparing sarvam-30b with GPT OSS 120B

Siddharth Srinivasan · March 2026

Would sarvam-30b make different ordering decisions on demand shaped around Indian automotive seasonality? I compared its completed runs with an earlier GPT OSS 120B reference. The ordering results were close, while run completion exposed a separate issue with the local serving configuration.

Testing seasonal behaviour, not cultural knowledge in general

I ran sarvam-30b locally through llama-server using the Q4_K_M GGUF version. It is a mixture-of-experts model with 30 billion total parameters and approximately 2.4 billion active parameters. The GPT OSS 120B results came from Version 2; I did not rerun that model here.

The motivation was to test a model developed for an Indian-language and cultural context on this particular ordering task. Agents saw the current month and a supply-chain role, but no year or explicit seasonal-event labels. The experiment could show how the models responded to those prompts, not establish their cultural knowledge more broadly.

The comparison I could complete

A fictional three-tier automotive supply chain served one product with fixed one-month lead times. The 25-month synthetic demand series represented Indian passenger-vehicle seasonal patterns, including festive peaks and monsoon troughs. It was not proprietary sales data. Each agent placed orders from its current numerical inventory state without remembering earlier decisions.

The comparison used ten completed runs in each of two context conditions: the native reasoning flag disabled and enabled. Both used temperature 1.0 and top_p 1.0, following the local GGUF documentation. Calibration did not achieve dependable completion for the numbers-only condition, so that condition is absent.

I measured stockouts alongside chain OVAR: order variance divided by demand variance at each tier, averaged across the chain. Below one means damping; above one means amplification. The practical-importance threshold was a difference of at least 0.5 OVAR. A separate pattern score tracked seasonal keywords and order-elevation behaviour.

Close observed results, not demonstrated equivalence

The observed differences were far below the 0.5 threshold, but no formal equivalence test was performed. Seasonal pattern scores were also close: 0.219 and 0.232 for sarvam-30b, against 0.21 for the reference. The expected seasonal advantage did not appear under these prompts. That is not evidence that the models lack seasonal knowledge in other tasks.

Neither sarvam condition approached the exponential-smoothing reference, which achieved about 0.54 OVAR with five stockouts. The GPT OSS 120B reference also had twenty runs rather than ten, so sample sizes and precision were not matched.

Completed runs do not tell the whole reliability story

With reasoning disabled, 15 of 25 attempts needed replacement to obtain ten completed runs. With reasoning enabled, none of ten attempts needed replacement. The disabled condition also contained a conflicting “Think silently” instruction, however, so the difference cannot be attributed to the reasoning flag alone.

These are integration observations about the local GGUF and llama-server setup, not universal properties of the model or its cloud deployment. The failed attempts remain part of the evidence even though the results table describes completed runs.

What needs testing next

Can the prompt conflict be removed while maintaining reliable completion? Would a different task framing or an agent with memory show different seasonal behaviour? This context-only comparison leaves both questions open. Any follow-up needs to keep serving reliability separate from the quality of the orders.
