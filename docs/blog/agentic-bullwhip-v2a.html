Comparing sarvam-30b with GPT OSS 120B

Siddharth Srinivasan · March 2026

Would a model developed for an Indian-language and cultural context make different replenishment decisions on a demand series shaped around Indian automotive seasonality? This experiment compared sarvam-30b with an existing GPT OSS 120B reference.

The model and the question

I ran the Q4_K_M GGUF version of sarvam-30b locally through llama-server. It is a mixture-of-experts model with 30 billion total parameters and approximately 2.4 billion active parameters. GPT OSS 120B results came from the earlier Version 2 experiment; that model was not rerun here.

The question concerned observed ordering behaviour under these prompts—not a general test of either model's cultural knowledge. Agents saw the current month and a supply-chain role, but no year or explicit seasonal-event labels. Any use of seasonality would need to come from how the model connected that prompt to its learned knowledge.

The data and method

The setting was a fictional three-tier automotive supply chain with one product, fixed one-month lead times, and 25 months of synthetic demand calibrated to Indian passenger-vehicle seasonal patterns. It included festive peaks and monsoon troughs, not proprietary sales data. Each agent received its current numerical inventory state and placed an order without memory of earlier decisions.

The canonical comparison used ten completed runs for each of two context conditions: the native reasoning flag disabled and enabled. Both used temperature 1.0 and top_p 1.0, following the local GGUF documentation. The numbers-only condition was not included because calibration did not achieve dependable completion.

The main outcomes were chain OVAR and stockouts. OVAR is the variance of orders divided by the variance of demand, calculated per tier and averaged across the chain. Below one means damping; above one means amplification. A difference of at least 0.5 OVAR was required for the study's practical-importance threshold. A separate pattern score measured seasonal keyword and order-elevation behaviour.

What we found

GPT OSS 120B, earlier reference4.52 ± 0.0539.6

The observed ordering results were close, far below the 0.5 difference threshold, but this was not a formal equivalence test. Seasonal pattern scores were also close: 0.219 and 0.232 for sarvam-30b, against 0.21 for the reference. The tested prompts did not show the expected seasonal advantage. This does not establish that the models lack seasonal knowledge outside this task.

The exponential-smoothing reference achieved OVAR about 0.54 with five stockouts. Neither sarvam condition approached it. The reference model had twenty runs, rather than ten, so precision and sample sizes were not matched.

Reliability was a separate result

With reasoning disabled, 15 of 25 run attempts needed replacement to obtain ten completed runs. With reasoning enabled, none of ten attempts needed replacement. However, the disabled condition also contained a conflicting “Think silently” instruction. The improvement cannot be attributed to the reasoning flag alone.

The integration observations concern this local GGUF and llama-server setup. Cloud settings are a different deployment surface. Successful completion under one configuration is not evidence of universal reliability, and failed attempts must remain visible alongside the completed-run comparison.

Next steps

The unresolved questions are whether the prompt conflict can be removed while preserving reliable completion, and whether a different task framing or stateful architecture changes seasonal behaviour. Neither was established by this context-only comparison. Any extension should keep integration reliability separate from the quality of the ordering decisions.
