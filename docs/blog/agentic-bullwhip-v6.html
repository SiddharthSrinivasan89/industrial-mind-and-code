Adaptive smoothing in supply chain replenishment

Siddharth Srinivasan · April 2026

What if AI adjusted how quickly a forecast reacts to demand, while a formula calculated the order? Every tested model-condition mean dampened order variance in this simulation, although the best result did not establish superiority over a fixed smoothing setting.

The question and the models

I tested gpt-4.1-mini, o4-mini, and GPT OSS 120B in an adaptive exponential-smoothing architecture. Each model selected α from 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, or 0.7 at each period and tier. This coefficient controls how strongly the latest demand observation changes the forecast: a larger value makes it more responsive.

The formula derived the order from the smoothed forecast. There was no safety-stock multiplier and no Order-Up-To calculation. The question was whether this bounded parameter-setting role could dampen variability and perform competitively with the same formula using a fixed coefficient.

The data and method

The fictional three-tier chain used one product and a 25-month synthetic demand series shaped around Indian automotive seasonality. Lead times were fixed at one month, with no stochastic fill rates or injected disruption events. This was simpler than the V4 and V5 environment, so cross-experiment improvements cannot be attributed to architecture alone.

Each model ran ten repetitions in three conditions: numerical state alone, added calendar and seasonal context, and a stateful version with recent α history. Four follow-up conditions ran five repetitions each. They removed seasonal guidance and instructed the models to use numerical evidence; the computed-signal variants also replaced the calendar input with derived statistics. All four α options remained available. Fixed-coefficient baselines ran once in the deterministic environment.

The main measure was chain OVAR, calculated as each tier's order variance divided by demand variance and averaged across the chain. Values below one mean damping. Stockouts were measured alongside it to check whether a calmer order series came with worse availability.

All nine adaptive means were below one, ranging from 0.535 to 0.741. GPT OSS 120B with numerical state alone had the lowest observed OVAR and averaged 4.4 stockouts. The fixed α = 0.3 baseline achieved about 0.545 with five stockouts. The 0.010 point-estimate gap is small relative to run-to-run variation; these results do not establish that adding AI beats the fixed baseline.

Context increased OVAR for all three models, and the history conditions fell between their numbers-only and context counterparts. This is a measured context penalty in the tested setup. The reported averages do not support a blanket explanation that context simply raised mean α: the context means were lower than the numbers-only means. The timing and distribution of choices would need separate evidence to establish a mechanism.

The published five-run GPT OSS 120B computed-signal batch reached 0.585, compared with 0.739 in the original context condition. Another stored five-run batch reached 0.573; the available records do not explain why the published account selected one batch. Both are reported here without pooling or selecting a new headline result.

The revised gpt-4.1-mini instruction condition reached 0.596, while its computed-signal condition reached 0.679. The latter was closer to the original context result than to the blind result, so the follow-up did not uniformly meet its stated closeness criterion. Its five runs also repeated the same reported α distributions; they should not be read as five diverse demand scenarios. These are bounded observations, not evidence that a general debiasing mechanism has been established.

Limits and next steps

The useful finding is that this parameter-setting interface produced damping condition means in the tested deterministic environment. It does not establish that a language model is necessary: the fixed smoothing formula already dampened variance, and its best comparison remains competitive without inference cost or integration complexity.

The next question is whether the small observed advantage persists with more repetitions and a broader demand or disruption setting. Those extensions are not results of this study. Conclusions remain limited to three models, a four-value option set, one synthetic demand series, and the stated run counts. The hypotheses were not externally pre-registered.
